Susan Klebanoff, PhD

Psychologist

I am a clinical psychologist and psychoanalyst with over 35 years of experience. I am in private practice in New York City. I work both in person in Greenwich Village as well as remotely via zoom.

Throughout my career, I have worked with individuals, couples and families. I have a particular interest in helping people suffering from loss, trauma, and family estrangement, as well as those going through major life adjustments.

Clinical Philosophy

As a relational psychoanalyst, I believe in the healing power of the therapeutic relationship, the importance of bearing witness, and the sanctity of the therapeutic space. I focus on understanding each unique individual: their psychosocial and relational histories, their family dynamics, and their patterns of behavior that may be interfering with their personal relationships or career satisfaction.

I believe therapy must provide a safe-enough holding environment for patients to explore their vulnerabilities. As singer/songwriter Leonard Cohen has written:

“there is a crack, a crack in everything, that’s how the light gets in” Leonard Cohen, “Anthem”

As a therapist, I am ready to listen without judgement. I am steady, grounded and conversational and can be directive when needed. One of my therapeutic goals is to help patients better understand themselves—their feelings, fears and motivations—so that they can move forward with greater self-awareness and inner calm.

I am particularly interested in the way the past lives on and repeats itself in the present. As William Faulkner wrote:

“The past is never dead. It’s not even really past.” William Faulkner, Requiem for a Nun

Writing

I have published and presented on a variety of topics including: Intergenerational transmission of trauma, immigration, eating disorders, family estrangement, adult sibling issues, creativity and unwelcomeness.

I am co-editor with Heather Ferguson of the forthcoming book, Revisiting Ferenczi’s Unwelcome Child in Turbulent Times (Routledge, 2027). I am also co-editor, with Adrienne Harris and Margery Kalb, of the two-book series: Ghosts in the Consulting Room: Echoes of Trauma in Psychoanalysis and Demons in the Consulting Room: Echoes of Genocide, Slavery and Extreme Trauma in Psychoanalytic Practice (Routledge, 2017). Earlier in my career, I was co-author with Ellen Luborsky of Ups and Downs: How to Beat the Blues and Teen Depression (Price, Stern, Sloan, 1999).

Education and Experience

I am on faculty at the Stephen Mitchell Relational Study Center, where I have served as a supervisor and mentor. I have also supervised interns at NYU Postdoctoral Center for Psychotherapy and Psychoanalysis and National Institute for the Psychotherapies (NIP).

Before starting my private practice, I worked on an inpatient unit at Long Island Jewish Hospital (Hillside).

I also trained at Eating Disorder Resource Center, one of the first outpatient centers in NYC to focus exclusively on the treatment of eating disorders. Before pursuing my PhD in Clinical Psychology at NYU, and training at NYU Postdoctoral Program in Psychotherapy and Psychoanalysis, I worked as a copy editor and free lance writer.
